# ADR-0105: Human views: eight task-driven, budgeted, deterministic projections of the weave graph, with counts that compose and no whole-graph picture

* Date: 2026-09-29

## Context and problem statement

The weave graph will hold typed, hash-anchored links over code, UI, diagrams, language, requirements, tests, proofs and evidence, and a checker will produce findings. The owner's goal is that humans understand exactly what a change did. A graph does not do that by existing. The evidence we could open says three things: there is no universally best display of a network (node-link wins path tasks and, with interaction, many topology tasks; matrices win group and density tasks and beyond about 20 vertices in dense graphs; an indented outline held its ground against four novel tree displays); understanding the change is the hurdle in review; and people ask "why" questions that they answer slowly by hand. The owner's own ProofMap Lite shows the failure mode of the alternative: each time completeness was made visible as a diagram, the next gap was that the diagram was too large to read, and another diagram was added (gap register rows GAP-051 to GAP-058, GAP-068; 20 sidebar model views, none budgeted as far as I could find). Without a contract, the human side of the graph becomes a hairball explorer, a set of unbudgeted dashboards, or model-written summaries that cannot be checked.

## Decision drivers

* Determinism doctrine D-01 to D-24: same inputs, byte-identical outputs; no clock, no floats in artefacts, no library default orders, no layout seeds.
* AI proposes, the deterministic kernel checks, the owner decides. UNKNOWN is never hidden or rounded up; NOT_RUN absorbs PASS; providers and agents never approve or apply (HCI invariants I1 to I4).
* The task decides the form (Ghoniem et al. 2005, Okoe et al. 2019, Kobsa 2004, Larkin and Simon 1987), and scale needs budgets and aggregation, not layout tricks.
* A container badge must be PASS only if every item is PASS, at every zoom level. MEASUREMENT (exhaustive, 9,330 sequences of length 1 to 5): the chain minimum `fold_b` has 0 violations; the weave join has 996 (it is the operator for repeated observations of one claim: `join(PASS, NOT_RUN)` is PASS). NEGATIVE CONTROL for a hypothetical hierarchical re-aggregation: the kernel `aggregate_status` fed its own output disagreed with the flat result in 12,504 of 25,488 splits and turned non-PASS into PASS in 3,720; the kernel never does this, so it is not a failure that occurs in EIJA.
* Explanations should be short, contrastive and free of probabilities (Miller 2019); questions can be a closed menu (Ko and Myers).
* No benefit is claimed until measured; budgets are HYPOTHESES to be calibrated by the HCI lane.
* OSS first (ADR-0016): renderers exist; only the projection layer is EIJA-specific.
* The HCI lane implements the UI; this record states requirements and interfaces only.

## Considered options

* **A. Explorer first.** An interactive force-directed node-link browser over the whole graph: Neo4j Browser, Gephi, Cytoscape.js or React Flow as the primary human surface, and generated diagrams for every model kind (ProofMap Lite's approach). Advantages: familiar, flexible, quick to demo, no view design. Drawbacks: whole-graph node-link is beyond the size where matrices win on most tasks in dense graphs (20 vertices in one study, hundreds of sparse nodes workable for topology tasks in another; either way the whole graph is far larger); force-directed layout uses floats and seeds, so pictures are not reproducible and cannot be a golden or a review artefact; Neo4j Browser and Gephi are GPL-3.0 tools outside the trust path; the ProofMap history shows the escalation. Rejected as the primary surface; kept as an optional user-run export target.
* **B. Eight task-driven projections with budgets** (chosen; section "Decision outcome"). Each view is defined by a question, a slice, a projection, a total order and a budget; outputs are canonical JSON plus a lossless text form; node-link is confined to a bounded neighbourhood with an integer grid.
* **C. Model-written walkthroughs and summaries** of a change or of graph communities (CodeRabbit-style walkthroughs, GraphRAG community summaries). Advantages: fluent, needs no view design. Drawbacks: not checkable, not deterministic, LLM-extracted structure is a proposal, not evidence; the HCI review dossier judges AI summaries as unverifiable review input. Rejected as a source of any view; an AI-labelled summary may sit beside a view (agents lane).
* **D. Reuse a traceability tool's own report** (OpenFastTrace, Doorstop, StrictDoc, Sphinx-needs) as the human view. Advantages: no build; OpenFastTrace defines the coverage vocabulary we reuse. Drawbacks: none of them has content-digest links with a code normaliser, soundness tiers, a ledger or our status algebra (dossier `[trace]`); OpenFastTrace is GPL-3.0 and a JVM process; their outputs would be a second source of truth for status. Rejected as the view; their reports may be imported as evidence, and StrictDoc can be an export target.
* **E. One dashboard of aggregate scores** (percent coverage, a risk score, a confidence percentage). Advantages: compact. Drawbacks: pseudo-precision; hides UNKNOWN; the impact aspect (ADR-0097) and the synthesis already reject confidence percentages and scalar risk; a share is `n of m`. Rejected.

## Decision outcome

Chosen option: "B", because it is the only option that is deterministic, budgeted, checkable against the compiler, and consistent with the evidence that the task decides the form. The decisions, each detailed in the design document:

1. **Eight human views** (`HV-01` to `HV-08`): change digest, ripple, trace matrix, coverage map, language tree, diagnostics, explain, neighbourhood. Each is a pure function `view(snapshot, params) -> document`; it stores and edits nothing; it reuses the agent contract's `snapshot`, `bounds`, `Finding`, `Step` and `Text` definitions and the same server-side functions. The HCI lane renders; the agents lane serves the same documents read-only.
2. **Form follows the question**: outline for hierarchy, roll-ups and gaps; matrix for requirement-by-evidence enumeration (rows sorted by `(area, id)`, at most 7 columns, 25 rows per page); grouped list for findings; one witness chain for "why"; node-link only for a bounded neighbourhood (20 nodes default, 50 on expand, hard stop 100). A whole-graph picture is not a view.
3. **Budgets** (chunks, disclosure levels, depth, rows, matrix cells, neighbourhood nodes, out-degree, witness segments, closure budget, latency target) are declared in the definition block of views.md with a stated basis, all HYPOTHESES; overflow becomes exact counts plus "N more" by priority-then-cap; filters report hidden non-PASS counts; a change to a budget is a reviewed diff.
4. **Counts compose; one badge operator.** Semantic zoom is a fold of status-count vectors, a commutative monoid. A container's badge (and every cross-claim roll-up) is `fold_b`, the chain minimum with PASS on top and NOT_RUN for the empty set, computed from the statuses present in the counts; it is PASS if and only if every item is PASS. The weave join (`fold_a`) is used only for evidence about a single claim and never for a badge. The chain (`chain_worst_first`) is data, hashed into `definition_digest`, and also fixes which rows survive a cap; display order is applied after the cut and cannot change a byte of a document; laws C1 to C6 are tests (C1, C2, C4 and C5 run today; C3 and C6 wait for the implemented views). The kernel `aggregate_status` is not changed here (kernel changes need their own ADR).
5. **Non-PASS is level 0.** Every view shows counts by all six statuses, the gap count and the not-applicable count; NOT_RUN and UNKNOWN are never drawn as PASS or as "unaffected"; an empty tier states what was searched; `snapshot.dirty` and `snapshot.not_run` are shown.
6. **Ripple has tiers.** Sound, over-approximate, heuristic and not analysed, as nested closures whose tier equals a maximin path; direct and transitive counted apart. MEASUREMENT on this repo: the sound tier is empty and 22 of 28 requirements are reached from `domain/impact.py` only through import-derived edges.
7. **Explanations from provenance.** One canonical witness (lexicographically first shortest path) grouped into at most 4 segments, a contrast with the baseline, limits, a closed set of question kinds, no probabilities, owner actions as text with `applies: false`. Absence is answered by the closed-world scope searched, or UNKNOWN when the closure was incomplete.
8. **Change digest is the entry point.** Operations in at most 4 chunks; chapters computed from layer, truth class and `derived_from`; a null edit (file digest changed, normalised digest equal) is counted under its hash method, never hidden without the counter.
9. **Determinism.** Documents are RFC 8785 subset JSON with integers only, no percentages, no timestamps; identity by id; no force-directed layout in any artefact: the neighbourhood carries an integer `(rank, slot)` grid and a user's drag is a layout complement keyed by id.
10. **View mapping totality.** Every node type and link type of `metamodel.json` is shown by some view or listed as `not_shown` with a reason (rule WV-028 in miniature; `tests/graph/test_human_views.py`). Overlap agreement between views (X1 to X6) is a set of tests: X4 and X6 run today on the reference definitions; X1 to X3 and X5 wait for the implemented views.

### Consequences

* Good: a person can answer "what changed, what does it touch, how sure are we, what is not known, why is this here" from bounded views whose numbers add up at every zoom level and are byte-stable; the views cannot disagree with the compiler because gaps and statuses come from rules and the fold; a hairball cannot appear because there is no whole-graph view; NOT_RUN and UNKNOWN stay visible; the definitions are executable (reference module and tests) before any UI exists.
* Good: a badge cannot show PASS over a non-PASS item (`fold_b`: 0 violations in 9,330 sequences; the join as a negative control: 996). The kernel re-aggregation experiment (3,720 of 25,488 splits to PASS) is a negative control for a hypothetical hierarchical roll-up, not a measured failure of EIJA.
* Good: it surfaced concrete data issues on this repo: the acceptance matrix `area` column has 22 values for 28 rows (17 singletons), so grouping by it is noise; 15 of 23 modules need the overflow rule in ripple lists; no declared `satisfies` link exists, so the sound tier is empty until links are declared.
* Bad: eight views are more to build and to keep consistent than one explorer; the consistency tests (X1 to X6) and the definition digest are the price. The HCI lane must give up a free-form layout for saved pictures.
* Bad: every budget is a guess. The evidence does not fix 20, 25, 5 or 7 for typed assurance graphs, and it pulls in two directions for node-link size. A bad budget shows as a review annoyance, not as an unsound result, because overflow is exact and counts are always present.
* Bad: an outline may not be faster than what people do today (no novel tree display beat an Explorer baseline in the study we read); the choice rests on compositional counts and low cost, and the HCI study measures it.
* Bad: the null-edit counter can hide a meaningful edit that the normaliser ignores; the counter names the method and reveals on request.
* Bad: with no declared links the views are mostly empty tiers and gaps (cold start). That is the honest state and it is the completeness view (HV-04) that says what to declare first.
* Revisit when: the HCI study or a calibration run shows a budget wrong (the study protocol is design document section 11; budgets then become target and limit ratchets as in the HCI lane's ADR-0040); the graph exceeds about 100 modules and the neighbourhood budget stops fitting typical egos; a treemap arm beats the outline for HV-04; a second platform run breaks byte identity of the bench; the status ADR settles the empty-slot value and the display order; the metamodel adds or removes a type (the totality test fails and names it).

Nothing in this record shows that any view improves comprehension. That is UNMEASURED and belongs to the HCI and evaluation lanes.
