## Problem and user goal

* Persona: P1 agent-change reviewer (primary, daily); P2 domain-model owner and P4 verification engineer (secondary). All HYPOTHESIS ([personas-and-jtbd.md](../hci/personas-and-jtbd.md)).
* Job to be done: when an agent changes the model, I want the change, its ripple, the evidence and every UNKNOWN in one view, so I can decide without hunting through a page.
* Task ids and frequency (hypotheses, `design/brief.json`): T02, T03, T06, T08, T09 daily; T01, T04, T05, T07, T11, T12, T13, T14 weekly; T10 rare.
* Problem, MEASURED on the baseline (Chromium, 2026-09-29, the real `index.html`, `app.css`, `app.js`): task content starts at y = 630 of 900 on all four tabs (587 of 768 at 1024x768) because a hero, a boundary bar, a case heading and tabs come first; page height is 1698 to 1863 px at 1440x900 (1691 to 1921 at 1024x768); Approve, Apply and the UNKNOWN box are below the first viewport at both sizes; on the Impact tab (T03, T04) 94 % of the importance-weighted critical elements are below it (scroll-free 0.059); the first viewport uses 10 to 13 distinct font sizes; one 18 x 18 px checkbox is below the WCAG target floor. The baseline is not over the word or container budgets (chrome words 77 to 109 over the ten baseline files, containers 4 to 9), so the failure is position and type, not volume. Position alone could be fixed without regions (option A' below: remove the hero, shrink the header, keep the status counts in a sticky strip), so this record has to show what the regions add beyond that; it can only do so with the study, not with the model.
* Out of scope: what is inside each region (other lanes), type sizes, colour, tokens, first-run layout, the XS (below 600 px) geometry file, native mobile.

## Quantitative model

* Model and formula. Pointing: MT = a + b log2(D/W + 1), D centre to centre, W the smaller side of the target (ASSUMPTION: MacKenzie 1992 offers it as an untested possibility; the CHI '92 test of 2D models it refers to was not opened; profile B, W = width, is the sensitivity check). Task time: KLM sum, K 0.20, H 0.40, M 1.35, B 0.10 s, R as stated, scroll as one K per page. Band plus or minus 21 %. Allocation: demand D_r = sum over tasks of f_t x c_t x a_t,r; widths = minima plus surplus in proportion to demand, with a main floor that rises continuously from 560 to 720, main_min(W) = clamp(560, W - 648, 720) (design file 3.2; an earlier step from 560 to 720 at 1440 moved the inspector 68 px for a 1 px change of the window). Layout vector: words, containers, depth, small targets, alignment, scroll-free share (design file, section 5).
* Inputs and sources. Constants a = 0.37 s, b = 0.13 s/bit (Cockburn et al. 2007); KLM operators (secondary source). The Card 1978 constants of sensitivity profile C are UNVERIFIED (not opened this session) and no conclusion rests on them. Geometry: MEASURED baseline boxes and specified proposed boxes in `design/layouts/`. Flows: design file, appendix B (same content in both flows: three typed answers of 22 characters, one acknowledgement, Approve, Apply, 400 ms kernel time, one reading pause per screen; the pointer starts at the viewport centre). Frequencies f_t (daily 5, weekly 1, rare 0.25), criticality c_t (1 to 3) and attention shares a_t,r are ASSUMPTIONS in the design file, section 3.2.
* Result for the current design, 1440x900, PREDICTION (scroll-corrected, meaning the first pointing act after a page-down is re-based on the scrolled screen; this is not a bound, page-coordinate values are 4.92, 5.56, 13.31 and 4.79 s; band plus or minus 21 %): T03 4.77 s, T06 5.56 s, T08 13.22 s, T04 by form 4.85 s. Weekly total over these tasks with frequencies 5, 5, 5, 1: 122.585 s. Layout vector, MEASURED geometry: containers 5 to 9, depth 2, chrome words 80 to 109, scroll-free 0.059 (Impact) and 0.500 (Evidence) element-weighted, 0.400 and 0.400 with each table or list counted once, alignment 0.762 to 0.875.
* Result for the proposed design, 1440x900, PREDICTION: T03 3.68 s, T06 5.54 s, T08 14.03 s, T04 by drag 5.21 s (4.43 s if the States lens is already open), by the non-drag path 6.74 s (5.95 s). Weekly total 121.468 s against 122.585 s (-0.91 %). At 1024x768: 120.379 s against 124.438 s (-3.26 %). Unrounded totals for every flow and viewport, including T04 at 1024x768, are in Appendix C of the design file. Layout vector, DESIGN COUNT: containers 0 to 2 (panels; plus 3 to 29 chips, whose border or fill is not decided), depth 0 to 1, chrome words 60 to 90, total visible words 120 to 159 (ESTIMATE from labels; baseline 126 to 171 MEASURED), sub-24 px targets 0, scroll-free 1.000 on all six screens, alignment 0.826 to 0.962. The 1.000 follows from how the layouts were specified (every critical element was placed in the first viewport); it is a design target, not a finding, and it does not score pane-internal scroll (3 critical items need it: the 125-cell Tests lens, the fully expanded 43-node tree, claim rows beyond nine). The fair comparison with the baseline is 1.000 against 0.30 to 0.40 group-weighted, not against 0.059.
* Option A' (design file 6.5), PREDICTION, scroll-corrected: weekly total 122.287 s at 1440x900 (-0.24 % against the baseline, B is -0.67 % against A') and 120.725 s at 1024x768 (-2.98 %; B is -0.29 % against A'). A' fixes the position problem in the model at almost the whole modelled gain at 1024x768. It still needs one page-down for T03 and T08 (scroll-free 0.600 and 0.833 on Impact and Evidence at 1440). B is 23 % faster than A' on T03, but that difference comes from content (a selection with counts against a JSON disclosure), not from the regions.
* Decision flips inside the band? Yes for any claim that the layout is faster: the aggregate difference is 1 % against a band of 21 %, and every per-task interval overlaps. T03 (-23 %) and the non-drag T04 path (+39 %) exceed the band size, but their intervals still overlap. No ordering flips between the four constants profiles (design file, 6.2). The decision therefore does not rest on time. It rests on the structural counts (UNKNOWN visible with zero operators against 0.91 s of operators in the baseline; A' also reaches zero with a status strip; zero page scrolls against one or two in the baseline and one in A' for T03 and T08; a persistent status bar; constant regions), on other lanes' requirements, on precedent, and on the study.
* Validity limits. KLM: expert, error-free, routine, mouse. Fitts: fitted on one-dimensional vertical moves of about 22 to 350 px (ID up to about 4.1 bits) with a mouse; the flows use two-dimensional moves of mostly 300 to 1000 px and up to 1074 px (ID up to 4.8 bits), which is extrapolation in distance and direction; keyboard paths (the palette, F6, arrow keys on seams) are outside it. Neither models reading, which dominates review. Allocation shares are a walkthrough by one author, not eye tracking; the demand model moves widths by 12 px, so the minima decide. Flows were written by the author of the layout.
* Deliberate deviations from the model. (1) "Review decision" sits after the UNKNOWN group in reading order, not in the slot Fitts would pick. The nearest of five candidate slots saves 0.03 to 0.16 s (PREDICTION, inside the band); we optimise time to evidence, not time to approve (P2, LAW section 3). (2) Approve and Apply are 24 px apart and only inside the decision dialog. (3) The non-drag alternative to a drag costs about 1.5 s more per use than the baseline form; it is required by WCAG 2.5.7 and stays.

## Decision

Chosen option: "B: five persistent regions with a status bar and a right-hand inspector". Reasons, in the order they carry weight: (1) the tree, the inspector beside a selection and a 720 x 520 canvas are requirements of the language, review and canvas lanes, and only a region shell hosts them without a page scroll; (2) B puts the daily tasks (T02, T03, T06, T08) and the UNKNOWN counts in the first viewport at 1440x900 and 1024x768 without a page scroll (DESIGN COUNT, by construction of the specification); option C has the same regions and status bar and does the same, so B is chosen over C on reading order (the ripple beside the selected row) and precedent, not on placement; (3) it costs no predicted time against the baseline (PREDICTION, inside the band); (4) it keeps region positions constant across destinations. What it does not show: that the regions, rather than removing the hero (option A'), produce any benefit. A' reaches almost the same modelled time and the same zero-operator UNKNOWN count, and the model cannot separate the two; the study can, and A' is a control arm. The evidence for user benefit is nil until the study runs.

Concrete behaviour and values (details and derivations in the design file):

1. Regions. Top bar 40 px (mark, case scope, destinations Changes, Model, Evidence, palette trigger, provider and egress state). Sidebar (model tree, depth at most 4, filter field, one status shape per row). Main (the lens). Inspector (ripple, coverage or properties; UNKNOWN group; "Review decision"; "Ask AI to propose" marked as a provider call). Status bar 28 px (six status counts in the order UNKNOWN, FAIL, CONFLICT, STALE, NOT_RUN, PASS, each a filter button, zero counts shown; attention set; stage). Overlays: command palette (640 wide) and decision dialog (720 wide), modal, centred over main, no blur, at most one elevation step. No separate agent panel.
2. The document never scrolls; panes scroll internally. Every element with importance 0.5 or more is inside the first viewport on the six proposed screens at 1440x900 and 1024x768 (a property of the specification; a built page must confirm it). At 1280x720, the template's other standard viewport, the same rules give the same result on all six screens (DESIGN COUNT from the lane's generator; the files are not published because this lane does not own a 1280x720 stem, design file 3.3 and O10); there the canvas is 632 x 576, below the canvas lane's 720 px width, which that lane states only for 1440x900.
3. Widths. Minima: sidebar 288 (PROVISIONAL, see below), inspector 360, main clamp(560, W - 648, 720), which is continuous (main takes all growth between 1208 and 1368). Surplus S above the minima by demand share 0.111 : 0.462 : 0.427: sidebar = 288 + q4(0.111 S), inspector = 360 + q4(0.538 S) - q4(0.111 S), main the rest, where q4 rounds to 4 px. Rounding the sidebar step and the combined side-pane step once each keeps every pane within 4 px per pixel of window change at every width from 1208 to 1600 (checked at each width); the first-draft rule, which rounded sidebar and inspector separately, moved main 7 px at 1457 to 1458 and would have failed its own pass criterion. Caps sidebar 480, inspector 560. In mode M the 40 px rail takes no surplus and main and inspector split it 0.462 : 0.427. At 1440: 296 / 752 / 392. At 1368 and 1280: 288 / 720 / 360 and 288 / 632 / 360. At 1208: 288 / 560 / 360. At 1024 (M): 40 / 592 / 392. The sidebar minimum rests on 6.5 px per character (a 13 px sans). The typography lane (HCI-ADR-0060, also proposed) sets 14 px and measures its sans about 6 % wider than Segoe UI, about 7.0 px per character, which makes the minimum 296: then 304 / 748 / 388 at 1440, 296 / 712 / 360 at 1368, 296 / 624 / 360 at 1280, L breakpoint 1216, and the 560 inspector cap holds about 76 characters instead of 80. All widths in this record are provisional on that one input; it is settled by measuring a built tree row in the chosen font, and the layout files are regenerated if it becomes 296.
4. Breakpoints: 1208 (three panes), 960 (sidebar becomes a 40 px rail), 600 (inspector becomes a drawer), below 600 one column with two-dimensional scrolling kept for the diagram and tables. Collapse order: sidebar first.
5. Seams: `role="separator"` per the APG pattern, with the required accessible name matching the primary pane (`aria-labelledby` to the pane's visible label, else `aria-label`: "Model tree", "Inspector"), arrow keys 8 px, Shift 32 px, Enter collapses, F6 cycles regions, Ctrl+B toggles the sidebar. Sizes are per-viewer preferences in `localStorage` (try/catch) and never part of the model layout or the presentation hash. Applied through `style.setProperty` on a custom property, with a data-attribute width ladder as the fallback if the CSP smoke test fails.
6. Density: Default (list row and chrome control 28; typed-answer fields 32) and Compact (24). Set from the palette and the `?` sheet. Rows of 28 and 24 are the heights the typography lane (HCI-ADR-0060), the language lane and the navigation layout already use.
7. Placement: frequent targets adjacent to the selection; interactive targets at least 24 x 24 (diagram handles exactly 24); "Review decision" after the UNKNOWN group; Approve and Apply only in the dialog, 24 px apart; the palette has no approve or apply row.
8. Scoring function and file conventions for the model package: design file, section 5 (`region-` ids are regions, `__` ids are virtual pointer anchors, `words` are chrome words, `importance` is normalised frequency times criticality).

Invariants. AI proposes, kernel checks, owner decides (I1): the inspector's Ask AI control is marked as a provider call and creates proposals only; the decision dialog is the only place with Approve and Apply, and it lists every UNKNOWN first (P2). Generated from the model (I2, P3): tree, ripple, coverage and pictures are projections; chrome layout is a per-viewer preference stored apart from model layout (ADR-008). UNKNOWN visible (I3, P1): six counts at level 0 on every screen, an UNKNOWN group in the inspector and the dialog, "not analysed" kept apart from "no impact". Providers and agents never approve or apply (I4): no such control exists outside the dialog, and the palette lists none. Untrusted text through text nodes (I5): layout adds no HTML path; the file conventions do not change the render rule.

## Validation plan

* Prototype measurements (one Chromium at a time; 1440x900, 1280x720, 1024x768; excursion fixture; both densities): scoring function over the built DOM; page scroll height; region widths at every window width from 960 to 1600 (adjacent widths must differ by at most 4 px per pane); target sizes and the seam spacing test; reflow at 320 px and 200 % zoom with text-spacing overrides; seam-drag frame timing; CSP smoke test for `setProperty`; keyboard tests for seams, F6 and Ctrl+B.
* Pass criteria, fixed before running: chrome words at most 120 per screen and within plus 10 % of the DESIGN COUNT; total visible words reported beside them (no pass threshold until the 120 target is calibrated); bordered or filled containers, chips included, at most 12; depth at most 3; font sizes at most 6; `scrollHeight` equals the viewport height and every element with importance 0.5 or more is inside it, on all six screens; widths within 4 px of the rule at 1600, 1440, 1368, 1280, 1208 and 1024, and no pane changing by more than 4 px between adjacent window widths, tested at every width from 1208 to 1600 and from 960 to 1207 (the rule meets this at every width); no width change of any pane when the lens changes; no interactive target below 24 x 24 except the seams, which must pass the 24 px circle test; p95 frame interval at most 16.7 ms and no long animation frame over 200 seam-drag frames; the fallback ladder passes if `setProperty` is blocked.
* User-study protocol: the study lane's protocol under `docs/hci/design/` had not been published when this was written; the layout arms are in the design file, section 10.2.
  * Design: within-subject, three arms: A the baseline Studio, A' the baseline with the hero removed and a sticky status strip (built as a variant of the current page), B the proposed shell; order counterbalanced (Latin square); T03, T06, T08, T04 on the excursion case. A' versus A estimates the effect of position; B versus A' estimates what the regions add. Without A' an effect of B cannot be credited to the regions.
  * Participants and sample size: 12 or more (the floor used by the dossiers); formative rounds of about 5 find problems but do not estimate prevalence; include a keyboard-only cohort.
  * Tasks: T03, T06, T08, T04, T14 from `design/brief.json`.
  * Measures: time on task; page scrolls per task; time to first sight of the UNKNOWN count; first-click accuracy on "where is the number of UNKNOWN items"; errors; UNKNOWN items misread as PASS (target zero); calibration; SEQ per task; SUS and Raw TLX with intervals; whether the missing agent panel is missed.
  * Analysis: effect sizes with confidence intervals; hypotheses pre-registered (median time to first sight of UNKNOWN lower than baseline; scrolls per task lower; B against A' stated as an estimate with an interval, no direction pre-registered); SUS compared with 68 only with an interval.
  * Stop criteria: stop and redesign if any participant approves with an unread UNKNOWN on a seeded item; if median time to first sight of UNKNOWN is not lower than baseline's; if more than 2 of 12 participants cannot find the UNKNOWN count within 10 s; if the accessible drag alternative has a higher error rate than the baseline form.
* Status of results: none yet. **No user-benefit claim is made until results exist.**

## Consequences

* Good: the daily tasks and UNKNOWN counts are in the first viewport at both sizes; regions do not move between destinations; the layout is machine-checkable from the JSON with one function; chrome words and containers fall against the baseline; 24 px targets everywhere; other lanes get one place to reconcile shell numbers (design file, section 9).
* Bad: the model cannot show that the regions beat a hero-less single page (A'), so the shell rests on other lanes' requirements and on the study; more regions to build and test than the baseline's one page; a persistent sidebar leaves the review lane's three-column review 1144 px, below its stated 1232 px (open item O1); shell words 24 against the content lane's 20; total visible words (chrome plus content) exceed the brief's 120 on most screens, as the baseline's do; the containers budget holds only if status chips are drawn without a box; the accessible alternative to dragging costs about 1.5 s more per use than the baseline form; a custom splitter and shell module must be written and registered; the layout says nothing about type or colour and so cannot be judged as "looks good".
* Cognitive Dimensions impact: visibility improves (UNKNOWN counts and ripple always on screen); hidden dependencies fall (ripple beside the operation); viscosity unchanged; premature commitment unchanged (no forced order between lenses); provisionality unchanged (AI output only in the inspector and the inbox, marked); secondary notation gains a rule (diagram node positions are model layout; pane sizes are chrome layout and stay apart); role-expressiveness unchanged; error-proneness improves at one point (Approve and Apply exist in one place).
* Security and repository impact: no new script or asset path. Pane sizes use `style.setProperty` (allowed only if the CSP smoke test agrees; fallback needs no inline style). Everything stays inside `app.js` and `app.css` (`/assets` allows only those two files). ADR-013 unchanged: no framework, no untrusted HTML. Layout preferences live in `localStorage`, never sent to the server. A custom shell and splitter module needs a row in `docs/oss/REGISTER.md`; OSS splitters were not evaluated this session (UNVERIFIED), so ADR-0016 requires that check first.
* Licence check: no fonts, icons or libraries added by this decision.
* Revisit when: the study shows the status bar count is missed (more than 2 of 12 cannot find it in 10 s) and a top-bar placement is worth testing; T05 or T04 becomes daily and the sidebar share overtakes the inspector's; the study or a second measurement shows the bottom panel (option C) reads coverage rows faster; the review lane keeps three columns; `setProperty` fails the CSP test; a built page differs from the DESIGN COUNT by more than 10 %; the type aspect changes base size or face (the sidebar minimum moves about 12 px per pixel of average character width; at 7.0 px it becomes 296); a cited source changes; the study shows no difference between A' and B on first sight of UNKNOWN, errors and scrolls for T06 and T08 (then the regions are justified by the tree and canvas lanes alone).
