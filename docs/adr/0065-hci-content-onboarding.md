## Problem and user goal

* Persona: P5 OSS evaluator is primary for first run (T10). P1 agent-change reviewer is primary for status, notice, error and decision copy. P3 requirements owner is the test case for vocabulary, because P3 may not know kernel terms. All personas are hypotheses.
* Job to be done: when I open the Studio for the first time, I want to see one real change reviewed by meaning, with UNKNOWN shown, so I understand the point without reading docs (J13). When something fails or blocks me, I want to be told what is unchanged and what to do (J3, J12). When I meet a domain term, I want its definition without leaving my place (J4).
* Task ids and frequency: T10 rare (decisive for adoption). Variants of brief tasks: T09-define-term and T09-define-term-kbd (variants of T09, find anything; weekly here against daily for the parent), T08-recover-stale and T08-recover-stale-kbd (variants of T08, with a mouse-only and a keyboard-Tab baseline; weekly against daily), T06-resolve-blocker (variant of T06; daily). The deviations from the parents are stated in `design/tasks/onboarding-flows.json`. Frequencies are hypotheses.
* Problem: the baseline carries 121 words of persistent chrome on every workspace screen (MEASURED; the whole 120-word budget), 10 eyebrow labels and inline explanations that every visit pays for. One act has three verbs (Authorise, Approve, acknowledged); "Approve" names two different acts; "Discard candidate" closes the whole case; "human comprehension" contradicts the kernel key `human_understanding`. Errors are a code, a colon and a terse kernel message with no next step. The first-run path is 5 required clicks over 6 screens and about 420 novel words (MEASURED). Opening the address without the private fragment leaves a blank chip.
* Out of scope: visual style of chips and glyphs; review chapters; ripple tiers; palette contents; the kernel changes listed in the design document, section 12; localisation.

## Quantitative model

* Model and formula. Four small models, all PREDICTION.
  1. **Reading exposure** T_read = f x W / wpm x 60 over the novel words W along a path (words not already on the previous screen). f is the fraction of words read: f = 1 assumes every word is read (an upper bound); f = 0.28 and f = 0.20 are the NN/g figures for typical web pages (sensitivity rows below).
  2. **Keystroke-level model** T = sum of operators M, K, T, P, B, H, R, for expert, error-free, routine tasks.
  3. **Break-even for on-demand definitions** p* = c_inline / L: the share of visits at which always-visible text and a click-to-open definition cost the same. c_inline = f x words x 60 / 250; L = M + P + B + R_open + H + K_esc + read(definition).
  4. **Readability** Flesch-Kincaid grade = 0.39 x (words / sentences) + 11.8 x (syllables / words) - 15.59.
* Inputs and sources. K 0.20 s (average skilled typist), P 1.10 s, B 0.10 s, H 0.40 s, M 1.35 s, band plus or minus 21% (LAW§2.4; S24). A chord counts 2 K. Reading 250 wpm, band 250 to 180 wpm (NN/g assumption; low-confidence lower bound). Local API call 100 ms each (ASSUMED, unmeasured); a command is 3 calls (POST, load case, refresh list). Verification 2.4 s (MEASURED mean 2.19 s plus 0.2 s for two reloads). Word counts: baseline MEASURED from `index.html` plus hand-counted dynamic strings; proposal = the section 8 drafts, which are design caps, not measurements, so the two sides are counted differently and must be re-counted with the lint on the built screens. Flows: `design/tasks/onboarding-flows.json` (T10, T09-define-term, T09-define-term-kbd, T08-recover-stale, T08-recover-stale-kbd, T06-resolve-blocker; all but T10 are variants of brief tasks T09, T08 and T06). H operators (device changes) are counted in every flow: 8 in the mouse-only T08 baseline, 3 in the keyboard-Tab T08 baseline, none in the T08 proposal; one on each side in T09-define-term. **Layouts.** The current-UI layouts now exist (`design/layouts/current-*.json`). The proposed screens of this aspect (HOME, Next slot, term chip, blocker rows, reload notice) are not in any layout file, so their P is the default 1.1 s. The headline numbers below use P = 1.1 s on both sides, a like-for-like rule. The flow file names a layout stem on the current P operators where both element ids exist in one layout, so the executable model computes Fitts times for those (a 0.37 s, b 0.13 s per bit, Cockburn et al., S28; W is the smaller side, D centre to centre); those are reported as a sensitivity because they bias against the proposal. Page coordinates are used and scrolling between elements is not modelled.

* Decision flips inside the band? Stated per part.
  * T10 action skeleton: the bands overlap (13.25 against 12.68; with Fitts on the baseline, 13.25 against 11.94), so the click saving alone is **not** decisive.
  * T10 total: the ranges are separate at f = 1, 0.28 and 0.20, but the separation comes only from word counts that are hand counts on one side and design caps on the other.   * T08: the ranges **overlap** against both baselines (proposal up to 28.6 s; keyboard baseline from 22.7 s; mouse-only baseline from 27.1 s), so no time benefit is claimed for T08. The case for keeping state in place is fewer retyped answers (an error-avoidance argument KLM does not model) and is a study item (H-ON5). The first version of this record reported non-overlapping ranges; that rested on the mouse-only baseline and on keeping the acknowledgement, and it is withdrawn.
  * T09 by click: ranges do not overlap (9.9 against 11.0). T09 by palette overlaps (11.6 against 10.7), so no claim.
  * T06: predicted equal within the band; no time benefit is claimed. The argument for blocker rows is error avoidance, which KLM does not model, and it is a study item (H-ON6).
* Validity limits. KLM covers expert, error-free, routine tasks; it does not cover novices, learning, reading with comprehension or error recovery. T10 is therefore a floor for a novice, not an estimate, and T08 models a known recovery sequence without further errors. The reading model applies one read fraction to both designs; Nielsen's 20 to 28% (S5) is for general web pages in 2005 (25 users) and the fraction probably depends on page length and task, so the two designs may differ. Word counts of the proposal are drafts. Local API latency is assumed. Fitts constants (S28) come from menu pointing with 8 participants; Fitts models are valid for roughly 2 to 8 bits (LAW§2.1, Soukoreff and MacKenzie 2004; S28 itself states no range). The current P operators span 0.58 to 0.96 s (ID 1.6 to 4.5 bits; the 0.96 s move is T06 approve to verify, so the auditor's 0.94 s omits it), and the two answer-field moves in the mouse-only T08 (1.6 bits) lie below the valid range, so the Fitts sensitivity is small and partly outside its model. Flesch-Kincaid is a weak instrument (school books; heuristic syllable counter, plus or minus 1 grade). p* assumes inline text is read on every visit at rate f and that the user finds the term chip.
* Deliberate deviations from the model. Approve and Apply are not made faster or nearer (P2; LAW§3); the Next slot never names or offers Approve or Apply. The decision surface is allowed 160 words instead of 120 because P1 outranks P10. Error messages are allowed to grow because the words are the recovery information. The blocker rows are kept although T06 predicts no time gain.

Why D1-a wins without a study arm: both alternatives break the one-word-per-kernel-act rule that fixes V1, and D1-c collides with the domain's own ambiguous term. The residual risk is measured (H-ON8) rather than designed away.

## Decision

Chosen option: "D: sample-first, state-driven copy, glossary from the model, copy classes and lint", because among the options scored it has the fewest words and required steps on the drafted screens (420 to 283 novel words, 121 to 19 persistent words, 5 to 3 clicks; counts of drafts, not measurements) while keeping UNKNOWN, reasons and bounds at level 0. The evidence for benefit is a model and precedent, not a study: the T10 time model is dominated by an assumed reading rate and the reading fraction the cited source reports is far below it, so no time saving is claimed for T10. It stays a proposal until the protocol ONB-1 runs.

Concrete behaviour (each bullet is a decision D1 to D8; details in the design document):

* **D1 Vocabulary.** The ubiquitous language in `docs/architecture/ARCHITECTURE.md` is the vocabulary (supported by Fowler for the shared-language principle only). **Hypothesis:** kernel words are printed verbatim in the monospace family at level 0. No source supports this in UI text and two of our own sources argue against jargon and obscure codes; ONB-1 arm C compares it with plain-word labels that keep the kernel word one level down (H-ON7), and the rule is reversed if arm A does worse. Registered short forms: owner, case, baseline. Retired: authorise, comprehension, human evidence, operation (semantic sense), candidate for a case. One verb for the owner's act: Approve, always with its object (`Approve revision 3`); the domain action is shown with its role (`Registrar · Approve`). Alternatives (Authorise, Sign off) are scored above; the revert condition is H-ON8. "Discard candidate" becomes "Discard case". UI prose is en-GB. A vocabulary lint runs on strings tagged `data-eija-source="chrome"`.
* **D2 Text budgets.** At most 120 visible words per primary viewport; DECIDE 160; persistent shell 20, which always includes the 3-word chip `human outcomes UNKNOWN` (I3: the scope limit stays on every screen, including TREE, RIPPLE and PAL, after the boundary bar and footer are removed); HOME 60. Copy classes: heading 5, button 4, status line 10, notice 10 words and 3 sentences, error 20 words and 4 short sentences with a 2-word outcome tag, empty state 6 / 15 / 4, definition 20, confirmation 25. Prose (text nodes of 8 or more words, excluding status, transaction and data lines) about 30 words per viewport. No eyebrow labels, no tagline in the workspace.
* **D3 Status grammar.** `<subject> <STATUS> · <reason> · <bound>`, with the reason from a closed list traced to `evidence.py`. Roll-ups print counts including zeros, never a percentage alone, and read `pass with unknowns` while UNKNOWN is above 0. "Checked and none" and "not analysed" are different strings. A rule's refusal is a result row with `role="status"` (SC 4.1.3), not an alert.
* **D4 Errors and notices.** Errors: outcome tag, what happened or is unchanged, what to do; a recovery control in the message where one exists; input kept; `role="alert"`; code at level 1; unmapped codes fall back to the kernel message and the code. Notices: result then what is unchanged; `role="status"`. Refusals: result rows with `role="status"`, announced without moving focus. Blockers are rows with their action. **Recovery keeps input, not consent:** `Reload case` after `STALE_VERSION` keeps the tab and typed answers whose question id and text are unchanged, and always clears the acknowledgement of UNKNOWNs, because the case version changed; the notice says `Acknowledge again`.
* **D5 Glossary.** Registered terms render as buttons that open a `popover="auto"` (fallback `<details>`), on click or Enter, never on hover alone; `?` on a focused term; `Define <term>` in the palette. Definitions come from term records (at most 20 words, optional "not the same as"). Untrusted text is set through `textContent`.
* **D6 First run.** HOME empty state (`No change cases yet`, one line, `Open excursion sample`, `New change case`); Select meaning; Run verification. The Next slot is a pure function of case stage and blockers and never names or offers Approve or Apply; at stage APPROVED it shows the status `Approved, not applied` with no control. No tour, no checklist, no modal. With a networked provider the sample opens the case only. TTFV-1 and TTFV-2 are defined; targets are hypotheses (median TTFV-2 at most 5 min).
* **D7 Measurement hooks.** Every string carries `data-eija-source` in {chrome, model, ai, provider, user}; a lint reports words per screen and per source, copy-class violations and banned synonyms. A test enumerates error codes from source.
* **D8 Dependencies.** Kernel reason codes (A4), the PREVIEW stage word, and the "comprehension" strings are requests to the kernel lane; until they land the UI prints `reason not reported` and shows kernel text verbatim.

Invariants and principles: **I1** the Next slot and every string are deterministic or provenance-labelled; AI is a label, never a speaker. **I3 and P1** every status keeps its word, reason and bound at level 0; UNKNOWN is counted including zero; the shell carries `human outcomes UNKNOWN` on every screen. **I4 and P2** no palette command or Next slot names or offers Approve or Apply, at any stage; the decision surface stays the only place and shows the exact revision and every UNKNOWN first. The first draft's APPROVED row offered Apply and contradicted this; the audit caught it and the row is now a status. A unit test over all stages asserts that no Next slot string contains Approve or Apply as an action. The acknowledgement of UNKNOWNs never survives a change of case version, so consent is always given on the state being approved. **I5** definitions, errors and provider text enter the page as text nodes. **I8** the sample never makes a provider call. **P9** provenance labels. **P10** budgets with recorded deviations. **P11** confirmations name what is not restored. **P12** every number is labelled.

## Validation plan

* Prototype measurements (one browser at a time, 1440x900 and 1280x720):
  * Word-count lint on the built screens by source: shell at most 20, HOME at most 60, every other screen at most 120, DECIDE at most 160; prose at most about 30 words per viewport.
  * Vocabulary lint: 0 banned synonyms in chrome strings; 0 unregistered variants of a registered term.
  * Copy-class lint: 0 violations of section 3 of the design document.
  * Error-code test: every code that the HTTP API can return either has a copy row or shows the kernel message plus the code; the list is enumerated from source (47 distinct codes today; 20 have rewrites).
  * Latency probe p95: definition popover opens at most 100 ms; `RUNNING` shows at most 100 ms after the click; the measured verification wall time (2.0 to 2.4 s here) stays in the 1 to 10 s class (Nielsen's classes, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/).
  * Re-count the T10 word totals (420 and 283) on the built screens with the lint and rerun `design/tasks/onboarding-flows.json` with the proposed layouts once they contain the HOME, Next slot, term chip and blocker elements; until then the proposed P operators use 1.1 s.
  * Unit test over every case stage: no Next slot string offers or names Approve or Apply as an action.
  * Unit test for recovery: after `STALE_VERSION` and `Reload case`, typed answers with unchanged question id and text are kept, the acknowledgement of UNKNOWNs is unticked, and Approve stays disabled until it is ticked again.
  * Refusal announcement test: trigger `ROLE_DENIED` and `ASSIGNMENT_DENIED` by click, by keyboard and by a drop; the refusal row has `role="status"`, focus does not move, and the platform screen reader announces it; a repeated identical refusal is also announced (Q12).
  * CSP smoke test for `popover` and `<dfn>` in one Chromium; keyboard-path test (Tab to a term, Enter, Esc, focus return); a screen-reader pass with the platform's built-in reader.
* Pass criteria, fixed before running: the numbers above; system-time check: a scripted run (Playwright, no human reading) along the proposed path spends at most 4.0 s in system response (3.3 s predicted with the assumed 100 ms per call, plus 21%); if it exceeds that, the latency assumption is revised before any claim is made.
* User-study protocol reference: section 11 of [content-and-onboarding.md](../hci/design/content-and-onboarding.md), protocol id ONB-1.
  * Design: first run between-subjects (baseline Studio versus proposal) because a first run cannot be repeated; T09-define-term, T06-resolve-blocker and T08-recover-stale within-subject with counterbalanced order. Arm C (plain-word labels with the kernel word secondary, tests D1) and optional arm D (a dismissible hint on HOME, option F) are extra between-subjects groups.
  * Participants and sample size: engineers who used an AI coding assistant in the last 3 months and never saw EIJA; formative rounds of 5 per arm find problems but do not estimate prevalence (LAW§2.10); about 20 per arm for the quantitative comparison, which gives a margin of about 19% of the mean (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/quantitative-studies-how-many-users/), so a proportion such as the 80% unassisted target carries a wide interval and is reported with it; 3 or more keyboard-only and 3 or more screen-reader participants as a qualitative cohort.
  * Tasks: T10 unassisted ("decide within 10 minutes whether this is worth trying"), then define a term (T09-define-term), resolve a blocker (T06-resolve-blocker), recover from a seeded stale case (T08-recover-stale), on the excursion workflow.
  * Measures: TTFV-1 and TTFV-2; first click on HOME; documentation opened; UNKNOWN misread as PASS (target zero); coverage-row reading probe; term-to-definition matching; Approve-versus-Approve mis-targeting; glossary opens; Next slot click-through; time on task; SEQ per task; SUS; Raw TLX.
  * Analysis: medians with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals and effect sizes; hypotheses H-ON1 to H-ON7 pre-registered (median TTFV-2 at most 5 min; at least 80% unassisted; zero UNKNOWN-as-PASS; vocabulary matching not lower; T08 not slower, with the difference compared with the predicted 5.45 to 11.05 s whose ranges overlap; blocker task not slower; verbatim kernel words do not increase status misreads or lower term matching compared with plain words, H-ON7; H-ON8 no participant confuses `Approve revision 3` with `Registrar · Approve`, else switch to D1-b and retest); SUS compared with 68 only with an interval.
  * Stop criteria: among the first 5 proposal-arm participants, stop and redesign if 2 or more miss the primary HOME action for 60 s, or 1 or more reads UNKNOWN as PASS, or 2 or more cannot reach TTFV-2 in 15 min, or anyone cannot recover from the no-session state, or anyone approves a reloaded case without noticing that the acknowledgement was cleared.
* Status of results: none yet. **No user-benefit claim is made until results exist.**

## Consequences

* Good: fewer words that every reader must pass; one place for each definition; errors that say what to do; UNKNOWN, reason and bound never hidden; a measurable content contract (lint and budgets); a first run that reaches PASS next to UNKNOWN in 3 required clicks.
* Bad: errors are 69% longer; the decision surface is over the 120-word target; the design depends on kernel changes (reason codes) and other lanes' screens; hidden definitions may not be found; the sample shows one domain and may suggest more generality than exists (mitigated by the scope popover); a lint and a term registry are new upkeep; T06 and T08 are predicted no faster within the band; the owner must re-tick the acknowledgement after every reload of a changed case, which costs about 2.55 s on purpose; the on-demand definitions have no time benefit if readers skip inline text anyway; the verbatim kernel words are an untested hypothesis that two cited sources oppose.
* Cognitive Dimensions impact: hidden dependencies fall (each blocker carries its action; "used in n places" in the definition); role-expressiveness rises (one verb per act, kernel words verbatim); error-proneness falls if the Approve collision and the no-session state test well; premature commitment unchanged (the Next slot never names Approve); visibility of UNKNOWN unchanged or better (roll-ups count zeros); viscosity of recovery falls (tab and answers kept in place) while consent is deliberately not kept; provisionality carried by provenance labels.
* Security and repository impact: no CSP change is required (`popover` is an attribute; no script, no inline style); `popover` and `<dfn>` need the CSP smoke test (SYNTHESIS C15). ADR-013 holds: all definition, error and provider text is set with `textContent`. A new client-side code-to-copy table and the `data-eija-source` attribute are the only new surface. No new dependency, so no `docs/oss/REGISTER.md` row. No telemetry is added (I6).
* Licence check: no font, icon, library or copied text is added. Definitions paraphrase this repository's own ARCHITECTURE.md; external sources are summarised and attributed, with at most a few words quoted.
* Revisit when: the study shows TTFV-2 median above 10 min or any UNKNOWN read as PASS; H-ON7 shows verbatim kernel words do worse than plain words; the Approve-versus-Approve mis-targeting rate is above the baseline or H-ON8 fails (switch to D1-b); measured p95 latency of the popover exceeds 100 ms; the CSP smoke test fails for `popover`; the kernel emits reason codes (replace `reason not reported`); the kernel renames PREVIEW; a viewport measurement shows a screen above its cap; a cited source changes; a second supported domain exists (the sample and the sentence "excursion sign-off only" change).
