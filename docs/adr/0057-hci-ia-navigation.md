## Problem and user goal

* Persona: P1 agent-change reviewer (ICP, primary). P2, P3, P4 and P5 all navigate; P5 (10-minute evaluator) meets the navigation first. Personas are literature-derived hypotheses ([personas-and-jtbd.md](../hci/personas-and-jtbd.md)).
* Job to be done: when I open the Studio to review an agent's change, I want to reach the change, what it touches and the evidence, with every UNKNOWN in view, and to get back to any element by name, so I can decide defensibly without hunting through tabs.
* Task ids and frequency: T02, T03, T06, T08, T09, T14 are the primary tasks; T01, T04, T05, T07, T10, T11, T12, T13 reach their content through this navigation. Frequencies are the hypotheses in `design/brief.json`: daily for T02, T03, T06, T08, T09; weekly for T01, T04, T05, T07, T11, T12, T13, T14; rare for T10.
* Problem: the baseline is one page with a hero and four tabs numbered as workflow phases. The tab strip starts at y 552 of 900 (MEASURED by the layout lane) and the first viewport holds 171 words on the Change tab. `04 / Evidence & Decision` merges two jobs and Approve is at document y 1406. The impact of a change is on another tab from the change, and tab 04 mixes reading evidence with deciding. `app.js` has no key handlers, keeps the open case and tab in variables, so a reload loses both, and destroys the focused case button after every load (COUNT and source reading). No element can be linked, searched or reached by name. The tabs lack tab semantics.
* Out of scope: shell dimensions beyond the stated interface, status glyphs and colour, review chapters and the ripple lane content, diagram interaction, the Decision surface internals, the tree structure itself, tokens, type, and any server or security change (the second-tab token problem is left open).

## Quantitative model

* Model and formula: KLM sum of operators (M, K, T, P, B, H, R); P from Fitts in the Shannon form MT = a + b log2(D/W + 1) where the layout is given, else 1.1 s; Hick-Hyman T = 0.24 + 0.08 log2(n) for stable menus of 2 to 12 items, used only to cap menu sizes; chunk limit 3 to 5 held items; WCAG target size.
* Inputs and sources: K 0.20 s, H 0.40, M 1.35, B 0.10, P 1.10 (KLM constants, Kieras; see Evidence). One B per click on both sides; Kieras counts a click as press plus release (0.2 s), so each delta understates by 0.1 s per extra click on the side with more clicks (T02: -1.40 becomes -1.50; no conclusion changes). Fitts constants a 0.37 s, b 0.13 s per bit (Cockburn et al., vertical menu pointing, mouse, 8 participants); D centre to centre; W the smaller side of the target (assumption). R 200 ms for a view change and 300 ms for a local API round trip (class targets of [HCI-ADR-0066](0066-hci-motion-performance.md), unmeasured). Pointer origin (720, 450), the origin the canvas lane uses. Geometry: baseline copied from the layout lane's measurement; proposal from `design/layouts/nav-proposed.json` at 1440x900. Flows: `design/tasks/ia-flows.json`. M policy: one M at the start of each cognitive unit and one before each variable string, the same on both sides (KLM rules 1, 2, 4); provider, verification and fixture waits excluded because they are identical; content steps owned by other aspects are identical constant-P steps.
* Result for the current design **PREDICTION**, constant P, band plus or minus 21 percent: T01 18.90 s (14.93 to 22.87); T02 5.60 (4.42 to 6.78); T02-kbd 5.40 (4.27 to 6.53); T03 4.30 (3.40 to 5.20); T04 8.20 (6.48 to 9.92); T06 5.30 (4.19 to 6.41); T08 18.65 (14.73 to 22.57); T09 6.30 (4.98 to 7.62); T14 4.10 (3.24 to 4.96). T05, T07, T11, T12, T13 are not supported (empty flow, not zero seconds; `design/brief.json` marks T13 partial because whole-request interpretations exist, but the element-scoped path is absent). T10 13.45 (novice task, not valid). **T09, T10 and T14 are workaround comparators**: `design/brief.json` marks the capability absent in the baseline, and the current flows (find-in-page on one tab, the create panel, scanning claim cards) do not reach the same outcome, so their deltas are not like-for-like costs.
* Result for the proposed design **PREDICTION**, constant P: T01 17.50 s (13.83 to 21.18); T02 4.20 (3.32 to 5.08); T02-kbd 3.60 (2.84 to 4.36); T03 4.10 (3.24 to 4.96); T04 6.60 (5.21 to 7.99); T05 9.20; T06 5.30 (4.19 to 6.41); T07 6.10; T08 18.45 (14.58 to 22.32); T09 5.30 (4.19 to 6.41); T11 12.20; T12 5.50; T13 14.30; T14 4.10 (3.24 to 4.96); T10 4.10 (step count 1 against 5). With Fitts P the totals are within 1.1 s of these; re-running the Fitts terms on the layout lane's own shell geometry (296 px tree, 392 px inspector) changes them by 0.00 to 0.07 s. Under Fitts P the proposal is slower by 0.36 s on T06 and 0.39 s on T14; under constant P both deltas are **0.00 s**. Both Fitts deltas are **EXTRAPOLATED**: the proposal's pointing terms on T06 (D 577 and 653 px, ID 3.95 and 4.42) and T14 (D 788 px, ID 4.86) lie outside the calibrated range (D up to about 340 px, ID up to about 4.0), and T14 is a workaround comparator. 22 of the 34 Fitts terms across all flows have D above 350 px and 11 have ID above 4.0 (COUNT of `ia-flows.json`), so the whole Fitts column is extrapolated and the constant-P column is the primary result.

With Fitts: current P1 D 616 px, W 85, ID 3.05, 0.77 s and P2 D 430, W 50, ID 3.26, 0.79 s give 4.96 s; proposed D 278, W 40, ID 2.99, 0.76 s gives 3.86 s. T02-kbd: current H 0.40 + M 1.35 + 3 Tab 0.60 + Enter 0.20 + R 0.30 + 4 Tab 0.80 + Enter 0.20 + R 0.20 + M 1.35 = 5.40 s; proposed H 0.40 + M 1.35 + Enter 0.20 + R 0.30 + M 1.35 = 3.60 s.
* Menu budgets (Hick-Hyman, expert; novice search in brackets): destinations 3 gives 0.37 s (0.54), against the baseline's 4 tabs 0.40 s (0.62), a saving of 0.033 s; lenses 6 gives 0.45 (0.78); attention groups 3; palette rows visible 8 gives 0.48 (0.94); tree siblings at most 12 gives 0.53 (1.26). Tree walk per level with fan-out 8: 0.48 + 1.10 + 0.10 = 1.68 s; palette lookup of a known name 3.65 s (3.25 with hands on the keyboard). The tree is as fast as the palette at depth 1 and slower from depth 2 (4.71 s against 3.65 s).
* Decision flips inside the band? **Yes for every task that both UIs support except T10**, so the time differences are not evidence. Over a grid of K 0.12, 0.20, 0.28, P constant or Fitts, with or without one extra M in the current flow (12 cells), separation occurs in T02 6 cells (all need the extra M), T02-kbd 8 (2 of 6 without it), T04 2, T09 1, T10 12, others 0. T10 is a novice task where KLM does not apply. No speed claim is made.
* Validity limits: KLM covers expert, error-free, routine tasks and not reading, learning, errors or discoverability; error about 21 percent; constants are from 1980 laboratory tasks and an eight-participant menu study. Fitts models one-dimensional pointing, and the constants were calibrated on vertical movements down a 16-item menu of 22 px items (D up to about 340 px, ID about 0.6 to 4.0 bits; distances derived from the item size, the paper does not state them), with a mouse, by 8 right-handed graduate CS students; applying them to 2D movements of up to 1157 px across a 1440x900 shell is an extrapolation; W as the smaller side is an assumption; the screen-edge advantage (NN/g Fitts page) is not credited. Hick-Hyman needs stable positions and practised users and is used here only to cap menu sizes inside the fitted range of 2 to 12 items; typed search is not modelled. The M count is a judgement. The T02-kbd current flow assumes Tab resumes at the removal point after the case list is rebuilt (4 Tabs; 6 if the browser restarts at the document start; not run). Proposal geometry is a design, not a build.
* Deliberate deviations from the model: Approve and Apply are not shortened or moved closer (P2; T08 differs by 0.2 s); the proposal keeps the status roll-up in the bottom bar although a top position would cost 0.02 s less (convention and persistence, PREDICTION); the words in the Review view are 133 against a target of about 120 (the launch view is 90).

## Decision

Chosen option: "C: three destinations with a stable frame, one palette, hash deep links", because it is the only option considered that puts each operation of a change beside its ripple (the baseline puts them on different tabs), gives three routes to any element (Borg et al.: searching, browsing, tracing; WCAG 2.4.5 as guidance only, since its applicability to a one-URL app is unclear), keeps UNKNOWN counts on every view (P1) and does so with conventions developers already know (Jakob). The model says it is **not** a speed win on mouse paths; the decision does not rest on speed.

What is co-visible in Review, and what is not. In the Review view at 1440x900 the operation rows, each row's ripple counts and its UNKNOWN count, the facet lane of the selected operation with a count and a gap glyph per row and a separate "Not analysed" line, and the six status counts of the roll-up are on screen together (`nav-proposed.json`, view `review`). Claim detail (bound, covered, assumed, NOT covered) is **one navigation away**, in the Evidence destination or through the Evidence lane row. So option C meets the NN/g tabs test for change and ripple, and only partly for evidence: the counts travel with the change, the claims do not. Evidence and Decision are separate places in C for a different reason than in B: the Decision surface is a blocking owner step entered only by Decide... (P2, I1), and it shows every UNKNOWN itself, so it does not depend on the Evidence destination being in view. Whether reviewers need claim detail beside the operation is a study question (Validation plan), not a settled point.

1. **Objects.** Destinations: Changes (change cases and agent runs), Model (the language tree and six lenses: Language, States, Journeys, Requirements, Tests, Personas), Evidence (Claims, Mapping). Agents, states, journeys, requirements, tests and personas are rows, lenses and facets, not destinations.
2. **Frame.** Top bar 40 px, tree sidebar 272, inspector 360, status bar 28 at 1440x900; slots keep their position in every view; the primary action slot at the top right of the main region (Decide..., Run verification, New change..., Run preview). The four tabs are replaced; every baseline capability has a home (design document section 4.3). `Try` becomes the mode Run preview of the States lens; the Decision surface is a blocking view entered by Decide.... In Changes (scope Baseline) the sidebar shows the baseline tree with no row selected; selecting a node filters the inbox to cases whose diff touches that element, says so above the list with a clear control, and puts the element in the URL (`#/changes/<element>`).
3. **Scope.** A Baseline or Case scope switch in the top bar is the root of the location path; evidence and review are case-scoped.
4. **Palette.** Ctrl/Cmd+K, `/` for command mode, a visible trigger. It searches the current scope and says which scope and how many items it searched. Deterministic ranking; no learning. Three command classes only: read (C0), propose (C1), job (C2), the classes of HCI-ADR-0064. 34 commands in six groups. Owner-only actions (approve, apply, take, refuse, undo, discard, clear suspect, select meaning, typed edits, layout) are pointer rows that move focus and activate nothing. The registry throws for any other class. The palette never opens the Decision surface.
5. **Keyboard.** APG tree, tablist, combobox, dialog and toolbar patterns; 16 tab stops by design; focus is restored by stable id after a render. Character shortcuts (`/`, `?`, `g` sequences, lens digits) are inactive in text fields and can be turned off (WCAG 2.1.4); Ctrl/Cmd+K and Ctrl/Cmd+B are unconditional.
6. **Deep links.** Hash routes `#/scope/view/element?params` built from stable model ids; `rev` names the case revision; filters are in the URL; push on scope, destination, view or lens change and replace on selection or filter. A hash beginning `#/` is a route, anything else the launch token. The Decision surface has no URL and reopens only by a user activation. A copied link never contains the token. No server change.
7. **Wayfinding.** Scope chip, location path, tab title `<selection> · <scope> · EIJA Studio` with the attention count, status-bar counts that filter, lane counts as scent, named empty and not-found states.

Invariants and principles preserved. AI proposes: the only AI command is Ask AI..., marked as a provider call, and its output appears as provisional rows in Changes. ## Validation plan

* Prototype measurements (one browser at a time, at 1440x900 and 1280x720): (1) recompute every flow from `ia-flows.json` with the executable model and compare with the totals above; (2) static test that the palette registry accepts only C0, C1, C2, that no handler references the approve, apply, select, edit, layout, discard or save routes, and that each pointer row only moves focus; (3) deep-link round trip for every route in the grammar (fresh load equals the state that produced it), Back and Forward, unknown id, stale `rev`, a token never starting with `/`; (4) focus test: after opening a case the focused element is the first row, a re-render keeps focus, at most 16 tab stops; (5) target-size scan: every interactive element at least 24 by 24 CSS px; (6) CSP smoke test that `hashchange`, `pushState` and `popstate` work under the current policy (UNVERIFIED); (7) latency probe with at least 30 trials: palette open at most 100 ms and results after a keystroke at most 200 ms at p95; (8) character-shortcut toggle disables `/`, `?` and `g` sequences in a keyboard-only session; (9) Ctrl+K and Ctrl+B conflicts in one Chromium, then one other browser.
* Pass criteria, fixed before running: checks 2, 3, 5 and 8 must pass with 0 failures; check 4 at most 16 stops and 0 lost-focus events; check 7 the limits above; check 1 must reproduce the totals within 0.05 s.
* User-study protocol reference: the full protocol belongs to the study aspect under `docs/hci/design/` (not present when this record was written); the arm for this decision is inline in [information-architecture.md](../hci/design/information-architecture.md) section 13.
  * Design: a tree test of the destination, lens and tree hierarchy (text only; NN/g method, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tree-testing/), then a within-subject comparison of the baseline Studio against the proposal, counterbalanced, on the excursion workflow.
  * Participants and sample size: tree test at least 12 participants for a quantitative reading, formative rounds of about 5 to find problems (LAW section 2.10; NN/g gives no minimum); at least 12 for the within-subject comparison, including a keyboard-only cohort. Personas are hypotheses; recruit engineers who review agent-authored change.
  * Tasks: T02, T03, T06, T09, T14 in both parts; T11, T12 in the tree test; T08 only to watch for rubber-stamping.
  * Measures: time to first evidence; first-click success; directness; wrong-destination clicks; focus-loss events; UNKNOWN misread as PASS from the roll-up (target zero); calibration; per-task SEQ; SUS; Raw NASA-TLX; time from opening the Decision surface to Approve and the share of UNKNOWN items opened before it.
  * Analysis: effect sizes with confidence intervals; hypotheses fixed before the run: first-click success at least 80 percent on each of the seven tasks (a task below 60 percent is relabelled and retested); the KLM difference on T02 and T09 is expected to lie inside the band; SUS reported with an interval and compared with the 68 benchmark only with that interval.
  * Stop criteria: stop and redesign if any participant reads a roll-up count as readiness to approve on a seeded UNKNOWN, if any participant reaches Approve by a route other than Decide..., if first-click success is below 60 percent on T02 or T06, or if focus is lost in more than 1 in 10 keyboard trials.
* Status of results: none yet. **No user-benefit claim is made until results exist.**

## Consequences

* Good: each operation of a change stays in view with its ripple counts, UNKNOWN counts and status roll-up (claim detail is one navigation away); UNKNOWN counts are on every view; any element is reachable by tree, palette or link; keyboard operation is complete and survives a re-render; links and Back work; the four tabs and the hero leave the first screen; the palette cannot approve or apply by construction.
* Bad: more to build and test (tree, palette, router, focus manager, registry); the Review view is at or over budget (133 words, 11 containers) while the launch view is under it (90 words, 9 containers); the mouse paths are not faster by the model, and under the extrapolated Fitts profile the status roll-up costs 0.36 to 0.39 s more on T06 and T14 (0.00 s with constant P; T14 is a workaround comparator); the palette and the character shortcuts are invisible to novices unless the trigger and hints work; a second-tab deep link needs the launch link until the security lane decides.
* Cognitive Dimensions impact: visibility improved (roll-up on every view, scope and path always shown); hidden dependencies reduced (ripple lane beside the change, "Not analysed" apart); viscosity: rename and re-selection cost fewer steps, but a rename is refused by the kernel today (ADR-003); premature commitment: no forced order between tree, lens and evidence; role-expressiveness: kind words on every palette row; consistency improved (stable slots); error-proneness: the palette stays read, propose and job, and a wrong-selection keystroke cannot commit.
* Security and repository impact: no CSP change, no `/assets` allowlist change, no new server route; the client remains one `app.js` unless another aspect changes the allowlist. Hash routing and `popstate` are untested under the CSP (check 6). The token stays out of links and out of the URL after launch; a second tab needs the launch link. Element names that originate in provider proposals are untrusted and are inserted as text nodes only (ADR-013). New custom modules need `docs/oss/REGISTER.md` rows: palette, tree, router, focus manager, registry test; `@primer/behaviors` (MIT) is a spike, not an adoption. The palette registry test is a new gate under the repository's local-hook rule, not a CI job.
* Licence check: no font, icon or library is added by this record. If `@primer/behaviors` is adopted it is MIT (https://github.com/primer/behaviors, opened 2026-09-29).
* Revisit when: any of the tree-test tasks falls below 60 percent first-click success; palette p95 exceeds 100 ms (open) or 200 ms (results); the study shows a time difference outside the band in either direction; a kernel change adds objects (tests, personas, requirements) or removes stable ids; a browser or extension conflict makes Ctrl+K or Ctrl+B unusable; the integrator resolves the palette edit-command conflict with the canvas aspect differently; the security lane decides a second-tab token handoff; any cited source changes.
